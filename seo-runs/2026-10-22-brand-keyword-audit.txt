2. sameAs — exactly what's implemented
What sameAs does
sameAs is a Schema.org property that lets us identify external pages representing the same organization.

For Viby, it helps clarify that the Israeli business at joinviby.co.il, its Instagram account, and its LinkedIn company page belong to the same entity.

Google explicitly recommends relevant official social profiles in Organization structured data to help distinguish one company from another. It is an identity signal, not a guaranteed ranking boost. 

developers.google.com



Current live implementation
The homepage's production HTML contains this Organization information (reduced to the relevant fields):

{
  "@type": "Organization",
  "@id": "https://joinviby.co.il/#organization",
  "name": "Viby",
  "alternateName": "וייבי",
  "url": "https://joinviby.co.il",
  "sameAs": [
    "https://www.instagram.com/viby.il/"
  ]
}



This is already valid JSON-LD. The missing part is the official LinkedIn company profile.
Recommended implementation

/* src/lib/site.ts */

instagramUrl: "https://www.instagram.com/viby.il/",
linkedinUrl: "https://www.linkedin.com/company/vibyil/",



Then update the existing Organization node in src/components/ProductStructuredData.tsx:

sameAs: [
  siteConfig.instagramUrl,
  siteConfig.linkedinUrl,
],



Do not create another Organization or WebSite schema node. We should extend the existing structured data and preserve its permanent @id values.

The LinkedIn URL comes from the public URL field in your connected LinkedIn page administration: linkedin.com/company/vibyil. We should verify that it opens the intended public profile after saving.

Google says Organization markup can be placed on the homepage or About page; it doesn't need to be duplicated everywhere. Our homepage already satisfies the main placement requirement.

I also recommend adding an ordinary visible LinkedIn link alongside the existing Instagram link, preferably in the shared footer or company-information area. That gives visitors a direct way to verify the company's presence; the JSON-LD supplies the structured identity connection.

Estimated work: a very small, isolated code change, followed by structured-data and live HTML validation.

7. A newly identified gap: two Viby domains
joinviby.co.il
Primary marketing site

Current canonical marketing pages, metadata and structured data.
myviby.co.il
Requires review

Separate product/login site with a public homepage describing customer retention, digital punch cards and other Viby solutions.
I checked the rendered HTML of myviby.co.il. Its homepage currently has a commercial SEO title, describes many of the same Viby products, and I did not find an explicit canonical tag or a page-level noindex directive.

It also links to https://www.joinviby.co.il/ rather than the preferred non-www origin.

This is a more substantial issue to investigate than simply adding a LinkedIn link. We potentially have two domains competing for similar branded and commercial searches.

However, the pages aren't necessarily duplicates, and we must preserve the app's login and customer flows. Google recommends checking actual canonical selection before deciding how to consolidate similar content. 

developers.google.com




My preferred direction is to establish distinct roles: joinviby.co.il should be the main product-discovery and sales website; myviby.co.il should primarily serve the product experience and business login. Its public homepage can remain accessible, but should have an intentional indexing and keyword strategy.

We should not automatically redirect or noindex the app's homepage. First, determine whether it brings valuable branded traffic and how Google currently treats it.
